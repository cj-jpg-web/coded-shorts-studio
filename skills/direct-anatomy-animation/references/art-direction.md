# Drawing and visual direction

## Recognition before decoration

Design silhouette, anatomical landmarks, scale relationships, and material cues before adding a face. Make a gloved finger read through fingertip, joint/glove cues, wrist/hand context, and credible contact orientation. A teal oval can read as a worm. Distinguish follicle contents from subcutaneous fat through shape, containment, location, movement, and color; yellow circles alone can read as coins or fat.

Test phone-size and thumbnail frames without dialogue. Ask an unprimed reviewer to identify the body part, prop, action, and affected tissue. Record actual answers if available; label self-review as weaker evidence. Use short anchored anatomy labels for identification, location, or changed state, without competing with faces/contact.

## Framing and connected anatomy

Fill the frame with relevant anatomy. Crop tissue naturally beyond the image rather than floating a small whole organ inside a large torso. Keep enough context to establish location/scale. Extend scene geometry beyond every camera crop; check widest and most offset positions for exposed rectangular edges.

Separate surface, wall, cavity/lumen, contents, and supporting context. Author correct front/behind ordering. Share contact coordinates across touching layers. Maintain wall thickness and continuous boundaries during deformation, not just at rest.

Use connected paths, shared anchors, or a common deformation field at structural joins. Distinguish intentional tapering folds from accidental gaps. Inspect extreme poses and cuts. Do not hide seams with blur, random texture, or heavy outlines.

## Color and finish

Create local value/hue hierarchy. Recorded reference combinations include warm skin against pale blue, cream walls around burgundy cavities, white eyes/dark pupils, and selective yellow/red/green/blue accents. Muted colors can work; universal saturation is not a cure.

Avoid undifferentiated peach/beige/pink fields. Give focal tissue, background tissue, prop, and contents distinguishable roles. Use controlled thin dark outlines, selective highlights, modest shading, and subject-specific folds/textures. Avoid indiscriminate gloss, glow, blur, gradients, and repeated procedural marks.

Give characters identity through eyelid weight, eye spacing, brow shape, jaw/mouth silhouettes, posture, and temperament. Maintain a coherent visual family without stamping one face onto every organ.

Inspect setup, contact extreme, cutaway, reaction, and payoff frames before full rendering. Keep a baseline comparison board when available. A polished board does not prove temporal continuity or motion quality.
