# Planning team and production handoffs

Use this workflow when the user authorizes production or a comprehensive plan. Keep story selection, planning, implementation, and publication permissions distinct. For planning-only work, stop with the blueprint and unresolved tests; do not start artwork generation or rendering merely to complete a checklist.

## Direct one film

Assign one lead director to own the brief, approved identity, integrated timeline, current artifact version, conflicts, and delivery. Plan each story's strongest presentation before choosing construction methods. Treat earlier rigs and sets as examples, not a constraint on the new film. Do not trade away a shot's meaning or quality to fit existing assets or an assumed turnaround time.

Launch actual specialist agents when tools and task scope permit. Do not claim that an independent team checked work when only the lead performed role-based self-review. If agent tools are unavailable, perform explicit lead reviews and record that limitation. Respect concurrency limits; schedule dependent work in waves instead of spawning every role at once.

## Give planning specialists concrete deliverables

| Specialist | Assignment | Handoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story and research | Verify factual claims, causal order, stakes, hook, escalation, and consequence; separate reconstruction from evidence | Script with claim/source links, uncertainties, narration draft, and story beats |
| Scene direction | Give each meaningful line a presentation that supplies new visual information; plan framing, reveals, scene changes, and connections | Beat table, storyboard, camera destinations, transition relationships, and repetition risks |
| Art and environment | Design story-specific cast, costumes, hero props, scenery, explanatory graphics, color, and lighting | Style specification, representative views, per-shot artwork list, environment/state matrix, and layer/depth needs |
| Character performance | Track intention, gaze, emotion, body language, joints, contact, anticipation, follow-through, and recovery across shots | Acting timeline, emotional entry/exit states, required poses, hand/foot anchors, and contact risks |
| Technical feasibility | Determine how intended art and action can be constructed; investigate layers, geometry, joints, masks, attachments, effects, and rendering | Construction plan, coordinate/ownership contracts, dependency list, capability limits, and focused proof tests |
| Editorial and audio | Establish information rhythm, narration cues, caption placement, reaction holds, sound cues, and the final frame | Timed rough edit, cue sheet, audio plan, and audiovisual continuity risks |

Combine roles only when practical and preserve the deliverables. Keep a final reviewer separate from the creator of the material being judged whenever agents are available. Assign by expertise and task, not by inventing different authority levels among equally capable agents.

## Build the shared brief before parallel work

Give specialists the actual user constraints, current script/beat IDs, relevant accessible reference files and timestamps, style targets, project scope, and their exact output ownership. Include the state of evidence: full playback, temporal samples, stills, reported claims, or unverified inference. Tell them to inspect the actual material needed for their judgment. Provide no intended verdict to an independent reviewer.

Give each writing agent exclusive file ownership or a bounded patch area. Use shared beat, character, prop, shot, and issue IDs. Route cross-cutting changes through the lead; do not let parallel agents independently alter the same timeline, scene, or approved identity. Require a handoff to state decisions, evidence, open questions, dependencies, and tests needed.

## Reconcile into one production blueprint

Integrate these project artifacts before full production:

1. Brief: scope, factual/comic boundaries, output requirements, style identity, reference targets, and user preferences.
2. Research and coverage ledger: source access, observation method, relevant time ranges, factual support, and unresolved claims.
3. Script and beat table: narration, viewer question, visible evidence, representation, action, meaningful change, and cue words.
4. Storyboard and timed rough sequence: composition, attention order, hero detail, reveals, scale changes, transitions, opening, and ending.
5. Art plan: character views/poses, environment states, materials, lighting, props, diagrams, and shot-specific production requirements.
6. Performance plan: emotional continuity, intention, gaze, expressive poses, joint paths, weight, contact, and recovery.
7. Feasibility plan: construction methods, attachments, coordinate spaces, dependencies, uncertain capabilities, and acceptance tests.
8. Editorial/audio plan: current narration version, provisional or measured cues, captions, sound events, and final duration basis.
9. Risk and audit register: owners, evidence, severity, next experiment, repair status, and review gates.

Keep one authoritative version. Link specialist notes rather than duplicating contradictory instructions. A prose plan is not evidence that a difficult motion or painted treatment works; label the required test as unresolved.

## Resolve disagreements before expensive work

Ask the technical planner to return feasible options that preserve the intended meaning when a construction risk appears. Ask art and performance specialists to assess each option. Let the lead decide and record the tradeoff. Test uncertain interactions early with representative artwork. Do not silently replace a complex action with a static character and a caption.

Proceed through brief/research → integrated blueprint → representative artwork → difficult motion/transition tests → complete animation/edit → independent review → repaired final export. Allow local iteration inside a stage. Reopen upstream decisions when rendered evidence disproves them.

After two attempts at the same fault without verified improvement, stop repeating the same patch. Diagnose its underlying design or construction, choose a different approach, and test it. This threshold triggers reassessment, not permission to deliver known defects. Preserve checkpoints and record the exact remaining blocker if capabilities prevent completion.

## Keep routine production internal

Perform reversible planning, artwork, testing, repair, and review within the user's authorized scope without repeatedly asking for routine approval. Give brief progress updates with findings and decisions; do not dump internal drafts unless requested. Ask only for missing information or a creative decision that materially changes the brief and cannot reasonably be inferred.

Deliver the final downloadable video by default, after the lead inspects the independent findings and the actual final export. Keep blueprints, audit notes, and reproducible source available internally or where the user's storage instructions require them; surface them when requested. Do not call a prototype final, imply unavailable checks passed, or hide a material unresolved limitation.
