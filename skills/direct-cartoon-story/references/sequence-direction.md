# Sequence direction from the reference study

Read before planning another short after the Marathon baseline. Treat these as visual observations, not a reconstruction of the creator's proprietary implementation or proof of audience retention.

## Evidence boundary

The November 2026 study inspected chronological frame samples from 13 distinct supplied complete video files (14 uploads; the two Grave Bell files were byte-identical). Eight selected 4–8 second windows received denser four-frame-per-second inspection. The package's 20 contact sheets are partial-sequence evidence, not 20 watched complete videos. Sampled frames establish staging and changes in representation; they do not establish frame-exact action timing, easing, or a normal-speed audiovisual review. Keep these distinctions in future coverage ledgers.

The old package's motion report contradicts its style summary about camera holds and turn construction. Treat its precise frame/pixel measurements as reported claims until independently measured. Infer visible depth cues, not an unseen exact rig. A 3D body is an implementation option; it is not automatically closer to the reference than a well-directed illustrated rig.

## Require these plan fields

Extend the ordinary beat table with:
- Viewer question and evidence.
- Representation: lived scene, prop macro, cutaway, miniature/map, comparison, or reaction.
- What changes during the beat, and what the viewer knows afterward.
- Hero object, contact constraints, gaze target, pose change, and motivated camera destination.
- Transition correspondence: shared prop, shape, direction, color, action, or a deliberate cut. Name which; do not call every cut seamless.

Use a representation only where it clarifies the meaning. Do not impose a map, diagram, or transition on every clause. A repeated location can work when an action changes its state or meaning. More simultaneous motion is not the objective.

## Audit as an editor

- Look at the preceding and following beats, not just a beautiful frame. Does a reveal supply information before its narrated setup? Does a camera move finish before the detail is readable?
- Distinguish a useful hold from filler. A still reaction can be stronger than a looping cheer.
- Inspect hands and props around contact, not only at the named impact frame. Reject penetrating surfaces, sliding grips, disconnected endpoints, and an unmotivated prop reset.
- Review turns at close range. Preserve head silhouette, facial placement, clothing thickness cues, scene tint, outline character, and the approved illustrated identity.
- Check full environment construction from the actual camera route. In particular, do not leave hidden ground or road geometry spanning a river/bridge gap.
- Preserve painted material variation, specific architectural features, warm/cool separation, and restrained depth. A blank low-poly set with camera motion is a motion prototype, not final artwork.
- Approve a short test separately for contact, transition legibility, acting, art direction, and audiovisual timing. A pass in one category does not pass the others.

## Experimental status

The original clerk/order/convoy exercise is a fictional mechanics prototype. It tests a timed stamp, a dimensional head turn, a prop-to-miniature cut, and a convoy stop. It does not establish the painted/outlined reference style, convincing human reaction acting, smooth continuous scale travel, or final narration timing. Do not adopt its geometry character as the new default style. Do not use successful export or frame validity to label it reference quality.
