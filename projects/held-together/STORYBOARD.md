# Held Together — direction notes

24 seconds, 24 fps, 1080 × 1920.

| Time | Visual action | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3.2 | Approach a curved forearm with an open wound; needle moves into the first stitch area. | Establish a real object and scale before going closer. |
| 3.2–7.5 | Seven interrupted loops appear while the wound edges move together. | Make the mechanical support visible. |
| 7.5–11.4 | Camera travels across the closed stitch line, exposing curvature and shadows. | Show depth and a stable repair. |
| 11.4–16.4 | A shaped skin window reveals stylized dermis, fat lobules and growing connective fibers. | Move from outside appearance to an explanatory cutaway. |
| 16.4–20.4 | Fibers accumulate; the skin window closes. | Illustrate repair over compressed time. |
| 20.4–24 | Threads leave; the camera pulls back to a scar and the original setting. | Resolve the explanation at the starting scale. |

## Reference lessons

The supplied Zack D. Films clip motivated continuity of subject, macro scale changes, visible thread depth, and visual cause and effect. This study uses a different arm model, scene, script, camera route, materials, and cutaway treatment. It is not a shot-for-shot duplicate. No reference footage or soundtrack is packaged.

## Limits to assess in the next iteration

The hand and tissue are stylized procedural forms. Knot tying and removal are schematic. Needle animation is abbreviated; this is not a clinical demonstration. The surface has procedural microtexture, not scanned skin. The camera rig and layered cutaway are reusable, but topic-specific anatomy still requires authoring and review.
